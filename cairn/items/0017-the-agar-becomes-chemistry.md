The answer to "is it software?" Real agar is not "nutrient"; it is glucose and
lactose and citrate, each needing its own pathway. A bacterium that evolves a
new pathway does not win a score — it opens a niche nobody else occupies.

Here the dish gains substrates: small computational tasks laid down in patches.
A cell metabolises one by answering it; energy comes back with correctness.
The genome of a strain that eats substrate X is a working program for X, found
by ecology — it arose when a niche was empty, it competes, it gets displaced,
it coexists. `soma/` becomes a library indexed by problem. And the seed stops
being a mood: the mind derives the chemistry from it.

The line that must hold: no global fitness. Substrates are food, not tests.
See CONCEPT.md, "What it must never become".

Done when: a dish seeded with a question grows strains that digest at least
two substrates derived from it, `biotic library` lists them with measured
accuracy, and a substrate dropped mid-run has been colonised at least once.
