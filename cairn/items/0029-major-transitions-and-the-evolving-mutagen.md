The hallmark of open-endedness the field has not convincingly hit: new levels
of organisation. Cells becoming colonies. Genes that travel on their own.
Lineages that change how fast they change. None of these are built; the
obstacles are removed and the dish is watched.

And the mutagen itself stops being fixed — it learns the dish's dialect, and
strains inherit how they mutate. This is the evolution of evolvability, which
Lenski's flasks also showed: several lineages became hypermutators and paid
for it later.

Done when: each primitive exists behind a flag, a 50k-tick run with all of
them on has been observed and written up, and the naturalist can name a
colony when it sees one.
